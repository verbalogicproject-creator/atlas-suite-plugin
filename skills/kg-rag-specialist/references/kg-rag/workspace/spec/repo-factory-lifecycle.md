# Repository-factory lifecycle

This contract describes a parent-controlled path from an unformed request to
a locally release-ready repository. It is a lifecycle for a
manager-orchestrated hierarchical workflow, not an unattended runner, a
provider binding, or authorization for a protected effect.

## Intake and approval

Before delegation, the parent acknowledges that records and candidate changes
may be retained locally and asks for the following Five-Dot intent, one item
at a time: **Literal Task**, **Strategic Intent**, **Boundaries**, **Task
Type**, and **Relevant Principles**. The parent presents each proposed item
with a revision and content digest; confirmation binds that exact revision and
digest. A raw idea cannot enter implementation until all five confirmations
exist.

The parent then performs read-only reconnaissance and obtains a separate plan.
It presents scope, architecture, affected areas, tests, risks, exclusions,
task dependencies, and declared authority for explicit approval. The approved
plan binds its own revision and digest and seals the complete authority set
with a revision and digest. A rejected plan returns only to confirmed intent:
the parent revises and reconfirms every affected Dot before presenting another
plan. An approved plan, or a proven source whose intended parity is confirmed
through the same intake, may begin the execution portion of a repository
factory workflow.

## Forward execution and evidence

The normal path is fixed and forward: intake, confirmed intent, planning,
approved plan, implementation, verification, and local-release qualification.
Only plan rejection returns to intent. Verification failure may return to the
approved repair owner for no more than two repair cycles. Tasks declare their
dependencies, accepted and forbidden scope, and acceptance evidence; cycles,
unmet dependencies, exhausted counters, or a third repair attempt stop the
run.

After approval, authority is sealed. A fallback route, tool, effect, or scope
change requires a new human approval of a new sealed revision. Every recovery
path—including session continuation, generic continuation, resumed goals,
checkpoints, conversation history, and repository or prose reconstruction—may
recover safe progress and perform read-only inspection, but never restore any authority. Before a protected effect, the parent names its exact target and
scope and obtains fresh active-session approval for that effect. The parent is
the sole control plane and retains questions, approvals, commands, and
dispatch. Children do not launch children.

Evidence for a test or command includes the approved exact command form,
observable outcome, exit status, relevant result counts or bounded failure
detail, and the criterion it proves. Narration is not evidence. Where the host
exposes durable delegation identities, the implementer, tester, and independent
reviewer are distinct; if that distinction cannot be observed, it remains an
explicit limitation rather than a claim of independence.

## Qualification and handoff

Implementation and documentation proceed sequentially. Read-only verification
may fan out only behind a declared barrier; the parent explicitly joins every
branch and assigns each confirmed finding to a repair owner. The final review
is fresh and read-only against the exact candidate and its evidence.

The handoff records a manifest of relevant regular-file artifacts, exact
commands and evidence, unresolved or deferred items, and the verified
implementation SHA. Any later handoff commit has its own SHA and must not be
confused with that verified SHA. A clean local candidate with passing required
evidence and independent closure is **locally release-ready**. It is never a
claim that it was merged, pushed, submitted for review, deployed, published,
or otherwise released.
